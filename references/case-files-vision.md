# Case files — `/vision`

War stories behind the rules in `commands/vision.md`. Each heading is pointed to from its rule.

## The search list nobody could check

**Potluck, `/vision` (2026-09-12), read from the project's `docs/vision.md`.** The market read ran three
searches and wrote them on one line: *"best potluck sign up sheet app 2026 no account"*, *"Partiful potluck
feature 2026"*, *"SignUpGenius vs Perfect Potluck vs Evite potluck 2026 review"*. Below them sat a table of
eight comparables and ten source links. Nothing said which search verified which product. Google Forms and
RSVPify are in the table, and no query names either; whether a result page covered them, or the run knew
them, cannot be told from the file. Step 3b asks the run to confirm the read *"cites real named products (not
from memory)"*. With that file, the confirmation is an assertion.

**The bound that was proposed and declined (#252).** The first proposal copied `/architect`'s rule: one
search per comparable. The owner's re-review of seven logged `/vision` runs across four test projects found
**1 to 4 searches per run**, so a bound saves nothing. It would also cut the wrong search: two of the
4-search runs spent a query on something that was not a comparable at all, what users complain about
(*"… signup sheet complaints 'too complicated' OR clunky"*, *"bill splitting app complaints friction …"*).
That is where a sharpening insight comes from. `/architect`'s safeguard does not carry over either. A
comparable left unchecked is visible; an insight never found is not, because the exit criterion is still met,
just by a weaker insight.

**The rule this earned.** Every search goes in `docs/vision.md` as one line: query · what it settled (the
comparables it verified, the insight it gave, or "nothing"). There is no count. A comparable named without a
search gets its own line saying where the name came from. Step 3b then checks the read's comparables name by
name against the list, so "not from memory" becomes something a reader can check. It is the same receipt
principle the playbook applies to companion reads (`MECHANISMS-ON-DEMAND.md` §Read receipt).

*Deliberately not changed:* the five-part north star. #252's item 2, splitting it around `/validate`'s kill
verdict, was declined by the owner. It would open a window where three of its five parts do not exist.
